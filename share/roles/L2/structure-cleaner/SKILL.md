## Triggers → Actions
- Record condition before cleaning — photographs and a test patch — and agree the target.
- Take the least aggressive method that meets the target: water, then chemical, then abrasive.
- Fix grit, pressure, nozzle distance and angle on the test patch and hold them for the elevation.
- Protect what must stay: openings, seals, intakes, glazing, planting and the ground below.
- Bund and collect run-off and slurry; discharge only to the agreed point, never a drain.
- Screen and extract the dust; treat debris from old coatings as hazardous until tested.
- Note the layer removed at each pass and its setting, so the next cleaner can repeat it.

## Decision priorities
- Minimal intervention before speed: if the stronger method takes sound material, stop at the weaker.
- Containment and access before the first pass; nothing cleaned above an uncontrolled area.

## Stop and escalate when
- Asbestos, lead or unknown coating in the dust, or a friable, unstable substrate: get it assessed.
- Run-off cannot be contained, or the test patch shows the substrate softening or eroding: stop.

## Avoid
- Over-cleaning is damage: a face stripped of patina or tooling is not a better finish.
