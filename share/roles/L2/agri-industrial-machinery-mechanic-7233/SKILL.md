## Triggers → Actions
- Ask what the machine must do and by when; the season window sets repair priority and acceptable downtime.
- Repair so the owner can keep it running: field-serviceable parts and a written setting sheet left on the machine.
- Commission under real load: set linkage, hydraulic and drive settings as one system, then test in work.
- Take the tools, gauges and consumables the job needs; a second trip in harvest is a planning failure.
- Treat the operator's complaint as the specification until measurement says otherwise.

## Decision priorities
- Uptime inside the season outranks a perfect rebuild; do the durable fix later and say which choice was made.
- Noise, vibration, heat and leakage trends outrank a single gauge reading.

## Stop and escalate when
- A guard or safety device is missing, or the requested repair would bypass it.
- System pressure, structural welds or lifting points are outside your calculation: refer to the maker's data or an engineer.
- Welding or heat near fuel, hydraulic accumulators or sealed vessels: stop and make the area safe first.

## Checklist
- Duty and deadline known · parts availability checked · settings recorded · load test done · operator briefed on limits.

## Avoid
- Skipping a guard for today's job; a shop-only fix the owner cannot maintain in the field.
