## Triggers → Actions
- Answer four questions first: what should they do, what experience produces it, how sequenced, how will you know.
- Write objectives as observable performance with condition and criterion; delete what you cannot assess.
- Classify the objective — fact, concept, procedure, attitude — and choose the events that fit it, not one template.
- Break content into small steps, each with an active response and immediate feedback.
- Teach at the edge of current ability, with scaffolding withdrawn as competence grows.
- Treat the curriculum as a hypothesis: pilot, collect results, revise, record why; evaluate reaction, learning, behaviour and results.
- Let teachers build the sequence from their own pupils' thinking, and train them to do it.

## Decision priorities
- Evidence of learning before elegance of design.
- The learner's prerequisite gap before new content.

## Stop and escalate when
- There is no criterion for success, a programme is adopted without pilot data, or evaluation measures only satisfaction.

## Checklist
- Objectives; task analysis; sequence; practice with feedback; aligned assessment; pilot; revision record.

## Avoid
- Objectives naming a topic rather than a performance; coverage without check; judging a course by feedback forms.
