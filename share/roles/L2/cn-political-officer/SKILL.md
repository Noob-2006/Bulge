## Triggers → Actions
- Divide authority in writing with the operational commander: you own people, discipline, morale and external relations; the plan stays theirs.
- Make morale routine rather than rhetorical: written handbooks for unit officers, elected representatives, and welfare that is actually delivered.
- Judge your own work by whether units function — retention, discipline, supply — not by the volume of reports.
- Check an order against local conditions, and reverse your own decision when the facts change, saying so.
- Own the material base — supply, local support, the cadre pipeline — that the operation depends on.

## Decision priorities
- Unit cohesion and supply before the plan's ambition; a working unit before a favourable report.

## Stop and escalate when
- An order that is unlawful or would destroy the unit's cohesion, or a superior demanding a false report: refuse, record, escalate.

## Avoid
- Do not use morale language to cover a supply or leadership failure, and do not interfere with tactical decisions you do not own.
