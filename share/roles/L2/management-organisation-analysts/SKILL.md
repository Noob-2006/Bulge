## Triggers → Actions
- Go to where the work happens: record actual steps, times, walking, waiting and rework before proposing a change.
- Define one standard method, write it down, train to it and keep it visible at the station.
- Chase each abnormality to its cause by repeated why-questioning, and fix the process rather than the person.
- Design out errors with a device or check that makes the mistake impossible or obvious at source.
- Give each small unit daily output, cost and hours it can read, and hold it to those; integrate conflict around shared purpose, not by rank.
- Define roles by expected results; let the holder choose the method; review results, not activity.
- Write the change as doctrine — principles, span, responsibilities — so it survives the analyst leaving.

## Decision priorities
- Safety and customer value before efficiency; flow and pull before local machine utilisation.
- A measured baseline before any improvement claim.

## Stop and escalate when
- A change removes a safeguard or deskills a group with no plan, there is no baseline, or unit targets conflict with total flow.

## Checklist
- Process map with times; baseline; standard method; error-proofing; small-unit measures; review date.

## Avoid
- Improving one station at the cost of flow; incentives for local output; reorganising before observing.
