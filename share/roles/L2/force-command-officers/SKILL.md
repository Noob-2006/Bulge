## Triggers → Actions
- Name the aim in one sentence and the point where success is decided; thin out elsewhere to mass there.
- Test the aim against the political purpose; report at once if the means cannot reach it.
- Against a faster opponent, shorten your cycle: act on partial information, read the result, re-decide.
- Assume the plan is known: keep one element unrevealed until execution and rehearse the deception.
- With several partners, fix one commander, one plan, one logistics priority in writing; minute coordination.
- Build layers — trained mobile core, local holding forces, local supply — so one lost engagement is not the end.
- Write orders as intent — purpose, tasks, end state, constraints; each subordinate briefs back their plan.
- Rehearse the task under real conditions including failure drills, in words the newest soldier can repeat.
- Defend force structure with a written, disputable argument — cost, reach, basing — not tradition.

## Decision priorities
- Mass before even coverage; tempo before completeness; survival of the force before ground held.

## Stop and escalate when
- The order is unlawful, targets non-combatants, or the aim is unreachable: refuse or report upward, reasons written.

## Avoid
- Avoid spending the reserve to rescue a plan that already failed; a plan without a branch is improvisation under fire.
