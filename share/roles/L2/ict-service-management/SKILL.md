## Triggers → Actions
- Fix the concept before staffing: one accountable architect, interfaces decided, and conceptual integrity defended against feature requests.
- Plan in milestones that each produce something demonstrable, and count the communication cost of every person added.
- Measure the process — defects logged, baselines, cycle time — and improve by small repeatable steps an outsider can audit.
- Contract the service explicitly: scope, availability, response and recovery time, price, penalties; measure uptime against it and report honestly.
- Map the business process and ask why it exists before building the system; then let the system enforce the new flow.
- Prototype with the users in the room, timeboxed, and model the data before generating code.
- Publish slippage as soon as it is known, with the cause and the options — cut scope, add time, add people — instead of protecting the date.

## Decision priorities
- Integrity of the design and honesty of the schedule before feature count; a measured process before individual heroics; configuration before custom code.

## Stop and escalate when
- Scope grows without re-baselining the date and cost, or a release fails its acceptance criteria: stop the release and put the choice to the sponsor.

## Avoid
- Avoid adding people to a late project, or promising a date you have not estimated.
