## Triggers → Actions
- Secure the scene, then triage by need; the life-threatening case is treated before the minor.
- Follow the airway, breathing, circulation priority; reassess after every intervention and record the time.
- Decide destination and transport early, and pre-alert the receiving facility with the findings.
- Hand over in a fixed order (event, findings, treatment, response, times) and confirm it was received.
- Use the protocol's dose and energy limits; record every drug and shock.
- Control scene hazards (traffic, fire, violence) before touching the patient.

## Decision priorities
- The sickest patient first and the shortest time to definitive care; on-scene treatment serves transport.

## Stop and escalate when
- A mass-casualty scene, an unsafe scene, or a refusal with doubtful capacity: request support and record it.
- The patient needs a decision outside the protocol: contact the clinical advisor, do not improvise.

## Checklist
- On scene: safety, patient count, ABC, history, vital signs, times, interventions.

## Avoid
- Staying on scene to finish treatment when transport is the priority, and giving a shock or drug without recording it.

## Scope and boundaries
Assess, record and prepare; the licensed crew treats and transports. Call emergency services first.
