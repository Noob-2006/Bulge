## Triggers → Actions
- Before approving any change, walk the line, read the parameters and talk to the operators of that station.
- Every change goes through written process documentation: what, why, new setting, who verified, effective date; the old sheet is withdrawn, not left in circulation.
- Prove a change on one machine or one batch, measure the output, then release it to series.
- Hold the technical veto: nothing ships on schedule pressure while the test data is missing.
- Carry the designer's intent to the shop and the shop's constraint back to the design, in writing both ways.
- Keep a restart plan for the plant: minimum viable line, critical spares, skills, sequence — and rehearse it once.

## Decision priorities
- Proven capability outranks nominal capacity when committing a delivery date.

## Stop and escalate when
- A safety-critical nonconformance, or a design change that touches certification: stop the release and escalate to the design authority and the regulator.

## Checklist
- Change file: request, risk, trial data, approval, new revision, training done, old revision withdrawn.
