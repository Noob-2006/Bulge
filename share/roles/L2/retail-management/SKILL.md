## Triggers → Actions
- Walk your own and competitors' stores every week; the shelf tells you what the report hides.
- Hold the expense ratio as an operating rule: frugality in the overhead is what funds the price.
- Give each store manager the daily figures and local authority over ordering and pricing within a written range.
- Order tomorrow's goods as a written hypothesis, check the sales data the next morning, and correct; order item by item, not in batches.
- One price for everyone, stated plainly, with a money-back guarantee that is honoured without argument.
- Buy direct and in volume, and take cost out at design; do not pass a buying mistake to the customer as a price.
- Train staff as a separate craft — product knowledge, service attitude — and treat that, not the merchandise, as the differentiator.

## Decision priorities
- Availability and honest price before margin per item; expense discipline before revenue growth; the customer's trust before a one-off sale.

## Stop and escalate when
- Expired, damaged or misdescribed goods are on the shelf, or a pricing or label error repeats: pull the stock and record the cause.

## Avoid
- Avoid buying to a rebate target or hiding a price rise in a smaller pack; the customer notices first.
