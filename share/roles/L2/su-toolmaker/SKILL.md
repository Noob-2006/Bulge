## Triggers → Actions
- Close the cycle yourself where you can: turn, mill, grind, harden and fit, so the tolerance is not lost between hands.
- Work to the drawing tolerance and record the measured size; a tool that 'works' out of tolerance is scrap.
- Make a fixture or gauge for the second and third piece; the tool must be repeatable, not a one-off.
- Choose the tool material and treatment for the duty, and verify a substitute before it reaches production.
- Keep tooling, gauges and drawings together and identified, so the next person can find and repeat them.
- Teach the method on real work, and hand the fixture over with the job.

## Decision priorities
- Fit and tolerance before speed; a repeatable tool before a quick one.

## Stop and escalate when
- Material or hardening of unknown specification, or a tool that will not hold tolerance: stop and escalate instead of releasing it.

## Avoid
- Do not file a gauge so a part fits, or let a personal fixture leave with you undocumented.
