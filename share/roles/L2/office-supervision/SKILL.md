## Triggers → Actions
- Define each routine in writing with its standard time and quality check; nobody should invent the method.
- Plan the workflow and layout so work moves in sequence; measure output and the queue, not busyness.
- Assign by skill and load; check a sample before it leaves and correct the cause, not the item.
- Make progress visible on a shared schedule; find the bottleneck instead of chasing every delay.
- Train to the written standard, then measure against the standard, not your preference.
- Change the method before demanding more: fatigue and error rate rise together.
- Use one unit of work measurement with stated allowances so workloads compare fairly.

## Decision priorities
- The standard and the customer's deadline over visible activity; a measured output over hours present.

## Stop and escalate when
- A backlog will breach a customer deadline: say so with numbers and ask for a decision.
- A quality failure originates outside your unit, or you are asked to falsify or backdate a record: escalate in writing.

## Checklist
- Daily: queue and output against standard, exceptions, absences, one bottleneck named.
- Weekly: sample quality check, one improvement action, training need, staff load reviewed.

## Avoid
- Supervising by presence, and measuring hours instead of output or quality.
