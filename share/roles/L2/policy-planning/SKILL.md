## Triggers → Actions
- Define the concrete common interest; build the smallest body with real power to act on it and widen the mandate only after it delivers.
- Plan from balances: quantify availability and requirement for the binding items, hold reserves, then set mutually consistent targets.
- Publish targets, projections and assumptions so implementers align investment to the forecast.
- Pilot the measure in one place and leave room for small experiments beside the plan.
- Liberalise in one published package, front-load the cost, protect competition independently, compensate the losers explicitly.
- Sequence the reform and say why: fiscal and monetary fact first, then prices, then trade and entry.
- Appraise each project before allocating — cost, delivery capacity, one measurable target — and reject the unappraised.
- Sell the plan by naming the fiscal facts and trade-offs, not by promising outcomes you cannot control.

## Decision priorities
- Consistency of the whole plan before any single target; credible rules before speed; compensation alongside efficiency.

## Stop and escalate when
- A target rests on unverifiable figures, financing is unidentified, or rules are being reversed mid-course: stop and state cost and risk in writing.

## Avoid
- Avoid piecemeal reversal: a rule changed twice is not believed the third time.
