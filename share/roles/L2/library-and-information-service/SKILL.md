## Triggers → Actions
- Define what the catalogue must let a reader do: find a known item, see a topic's holdings, choose an edition.
- Give every item one consistent primary entry and cross-reference the other names it is known by.
- Classify by facets of the subject, and allow a new subject to fit without renumbering the shelves.
- Apply the same rules to every item, donations included; no local exception without a note.
- Catalogue at the level of the document, not the carrier, and index its parts where readers will look for them.
- Adapt classification and rules to the language and publishing practice of the collection, and record the adaptation.

## Decision priorities
- A reader's access before the tidiness of the scheme; consistency of the record before local preference.

## Stop and escalate when
- A request needs personal borrowing or search records beyond the retention policy: refuse and cite the policy.
- A classification decision would mislead readers about the subject: flag it for review rather than force a fit.
- Catalogue data came from an unverified source or a vendor batch: verify a sample before loading it.

## Checklist
- Reader task, entry rule, classification fit, record consistency, access limits.

## Avoid
- No private shelf arrangement, no item without a record, no subject heading invented for one book.
