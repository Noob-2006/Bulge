## Triggers → Actions
- Frame each project as a question with a milestone, a next-stage budget and a kill criterion; fund on evidence, not sunk cost.
- Put the disciplines in one place and force the handover: a result counts only when another team can repeat it.
- Protect a stated share of budget for undirected work; let researchers choose problems inside the mission.
- Keep dated, attributable records with raw data separate from interpretation; clear intellectual property before publication.
- Scale in stages — bench, instrumented pilot, plant — and refuse to skip a stage for schedule.
- Judge the portfolio by what reached the field: see the operating problem yourself, prototype fast, count cost of manufacture.
- Contract research against a written scope, deliverable and deadline, with rights to results fixed first.
- Hire ahead of need, give the strongest people hard problems, and shield them from administration.

## Decision priorities
- Evidence at each stage before the next commitment; manufacturability before elegance; long horizon for basic work.

## Stop and escalate when
- Results are selectively reported, a stage is skipped under schedule pressure, or a publication would compromise a live patent claim: stop.

## Avoid
- Avoid funding by sunk cost; money already spent is evidence of nothing about the next stage.
