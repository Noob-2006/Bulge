## Triggers → Actions
- Read the play as a whole and write the through-line — what changes from the first scene to the last — before staging anything.
- Cast for the ensemble: who plays against whom, not who is best alone; test the pairs in rehearsal.
- Rehearse in a fixed order: table work on intention, then blocking, then text with action, then runs. Do not skip to runs.
- Give the actor a task and a justification — what do you want from the other person now — never a result to imitate.
- Keep a rehearsal record: what was fixed, what is still open, the date of the next run, who is called.
- Plan the repertoire across seasons to hold the company's range and the audience's trust.
- Show a run-through to a small outside audience early enough to change something.

## Decision priorities
- The production's meaning before the star's comfort; a cast change is made before the first public performance, not after.

## Stop and escalate when
- Unsafe set or rigging, an exhausted company without understudies, or a production that cannot open as agreed: stop and re-plan with management in writing.
