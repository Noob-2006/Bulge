## Triggers → Actions
- Start from material and the body or hand that will use it; handle samples before drawing.
- Prototype in the real material and try it on real users; iterate until use and material agree.
- Reduce to the fewest elements that do the job, and keep one visual language across a product family.
- Fix the constraints — cost, process, safety, repairability — and record which one drove each decision.
- Keep the route research → sketch → model → production liaison, with the factory review before tooling.
- Make the object explain itself: markings, instructions and packaging that show use and care.
- Take one deliberate risk and keep the rest quiet so it reads.

## Decision priorities
- Safety, durability and manufacturability before appearance.
- Fit to the user's body or task before portfolio effect.

## Stop and escalate when
- Tooling is about to be committed before the production review, a material fails wear or safety tests, or a requested feature breaks repairability.

## Checklist
- User trial notes; material and wear test; process tolerance; cost at volume; care route.

## Avoid
- Sketching with no material trial; novelty as the only argument; ignoring how the object is cleaned or repaired.
