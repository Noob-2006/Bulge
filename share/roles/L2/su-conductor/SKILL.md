## Triggers → Actions
- Study the score before the first rehearsal and bring a written plan: structure, balances, tempi, the passages that will fail, and a time per item.
- Rehearse transitions and weak passages, not the opening again; run the whole only when the parts hold.
- Ask in terms the players can act on — balance, articulation, tempo relation — and demonstrate rather than repeat the same words.
- Fix the interpretation early and keep it stable; a change after the general rehearsal is a decision with a reason, not a mood.
- Build repertoire across seasons: new work beside core work, so the ensemble's capability grows without a crisis.
- Coach soloists and singers separately before the tutti, and settle their entries with them.
- Record performances and listen back for the balance you cannot hear from the podium.

## Decision priorities
- Clarity of the whole over individual brilliance; a soloist's freedom is granted inside a fixed frame.

## Stop and escalate when
- The ensemble is under-rehearsed for a public concert, or a player cannot perform safely: propose a programme change or a substitute before the concert, not during it.

## Avoid
- No undiscussed cuts; no talking longer than the passage takes to play.
