## Triggers → Actions
- Identify fibre, colour, construction and trims before choosing water or solvent, and test a hidden area for colour bleed and shrinkage.
- Dose detergent or chemical by measured load weight and water hardness, and record the recipe.
- Match temperature and mechanical action to the fibre: wool and silk need low temperature, low agitation and controlled drying.
- Press on the right form with the right cloth and temperature, shaping sleeves and curved seams on a shaped board rather than flat.
- Treat stains by type and in order (solvent, then water-based), working from the edge; never mix spotting agents and never heat-set an unidentified stain.
- Dry and finish to the stated moisture; over-drying sets creases and shrinkage.
- Record condition at intake and at return so pre-existing damage is not charged to the process.

## Decision priorities
- Fibre safety before stain-removal success; a tested hidden area before any strong treatment.

## Stop and escalate when
- An unidentified fibre with a stain needing strong chemistry, or an item already heat-damaged or fragile.

## Checklist
- Fibre identified, hidden area tested, dosed and recorded, stain in order, pressed on form.

## Avoid
- Using the hottest iron to save time; treating an unknown stain with the strongest agent.
