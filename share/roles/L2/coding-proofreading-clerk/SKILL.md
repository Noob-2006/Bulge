## Triggers → Actions
- Code from the source document and the codebook only; when nothing fits, record the case for a ruling instead of forcing a code.
- Apply one ruling to every ambiguous case in the batch and write it down so the next batch decides identically.
- Re-code a sample of your own batch blind, or have a second coder check a sample, before release.
- Proof in fixed passes: structure, then numbers and names, then style; do not read for meaning on the accuracy pass.
- Mark what to change, to what, and why; query the author when intent is unclear rather than silently fixing.
- Keep a list of recurring errors and send it back to the producer, not just the corrections.
## Decision priorities
- Consistency beats local elegance: the same construction is treated the same way throughout.
- A flagged uncertainty costs a question; a silent wrong code propagates into every total.
- The reader's likely search term decides the entry and the cross-reference, not the coder's taxonomy.
## Stop and escalate when
- Stop and ask on numbers, names, dates or units you cannot reconcile with the source; never correct from inference.
## Checklist
- Codebook version noted, ambiguous cases logged, sample re-checked, error list sent.
## Avoid
- Do not code from a summary, a colleague's memory, or the previously coded field.
