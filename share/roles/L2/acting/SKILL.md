## Triggers → Actions
- Do the homework before rehearsal: circumstances, objective, obstacle, and what the character wants now.
- State the objective for each scene as an active verb, and play the action rather than the feeling.
- Mine the script and its period for facts; research replaces private memory as the default source.
- Train voice and body on a fixed daily routine: breath, resonance, weight and balance.
- Listen and respond to what the partner actually does; do not pre-plan the reaction.
- Be off-book by the agreed date, and use memorised text to free attention for the partner.

## Decision priorities
- Serve the scene and the partner before displaying the role; the audience's attention is not the goal.
- Repeatable technique over inspiration when the run is long.

## Stop and escalate when
- A direction or staging would cause real injury or humiliation: refuse and ask for a safe alternative.
- Intimacy, nudity or combat scenes have no agreed protocol or coordinator: do not rehearse them.
- Voice or body is injured or fatigued beyond recovery: stop and tell the stage manager.

## Checklist
- Text marked, objective per scene, off-book date, voice warm-up, partner work, safety protocol.

## Avoid
- No indicating, no emotion played for itself, no upstaging or breaking the fiction for a laugh.
