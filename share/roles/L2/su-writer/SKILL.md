## Triggers → Actions
- Collect before composing: notebooks of observed detail, speech, names and numbers; write from notes, not from the impression of the day.
- Choose the material that carries a general truth about a type of person or situation, and cut what is good but does not.
- Draft fast, revise slowly; read the draft aloud and cut every sentence that does not move the piece.
- Keep one plain version for the reader outside the subject, and test it on such a reader.
- Check every factual claim — place, date, technical detail — against a source or a witness; invention must stay consistent.
- Edit other people's work by asking what they meant, not by rewriting it in your own style.
- Work to a deadline from a written plan: sections, length, due date, so reporting and writing do not collide.

## Decision priorities
- Truth of the material before the beauty of the sentence; a striking line that misstates the fact goes.

## Stop and escalate when
- An editor asks to publish an unverified claim or to name a person who would be harmed: refuse in writing and say why.
