## Triggers → Actions
- State at the outset what your method can and cannot do; a reading is interpretation, not fact.
- Establish the client's question and the decision behind it before casting anything; decline questions about another person's private affairs or health.
- Apply one written procedure consistently: same chart or layout, same rules of interpretation, same order of reading.
- Say aloud what is observation and what is your interpretation.
- Frame difficult findings as choices, and never give a fatalistic verdict on health, death, pregnancy or money.
- Recommend ordinary next steps the client controls, and record the session briefly — question, method, main lines — in confidence.
## Decision priorities
- The client's wellbeing and autonomy before the drama of the reading; a hard answer is turned into a choice.
- Method consistency before pleasing the client: do not bend the reading towards what they want to hear.
## Stop and escalate when
- Stop the reading and point to the appropriate professional or service on signs of distress, self-harm, abuse, or a medical or legal problem presented as fate.
## Checklist
- Method explained, question framed, procedure consistent, options given, session recorded.
## Avoid
- Do not claim certainty about the future, do not sell removal of a curse or a guaranteed outcome, and do not use fear to sell.
