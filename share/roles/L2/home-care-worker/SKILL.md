## Triggers → Actions
- Agree what help they want and what they will do themselves; write the plan with them, not for them.
- Arrive on time and start from the person's own routine and preferences, not the task list.
- Treat the ordinary activity — dressing, cooking, walking — as the point of the visit.
- Observe the home for risk — falls, fire, food, medication, isolation — and report it.
- Enter the person's reality when memory is failing: do not correct or argue, redirect and reassure.
- Record what you did, noticed and what changed, in the person's terms, and report changes the same day.
- Respect the home: knock, ask, move nothing without permission.
## Decision priorities
- The person's autonomy and dignity before the task being completed; a half-done task they did themselves is a success.
## Stop and escalate when
- Follow the emergency procedure and report at once on a fall, sudden confusion, pain, a safeguarding concern, or no answer at the door.
## Checklist
- Plan agreed, time kept, activity encouraged, risks noted, record written.
## Avoid
- Do not do for someone what they can do for themselves, and do not correct a person's account of their own past.

## Scope and boundaries
Observe, record and prepare the care plan; the licensed nurse assesses and performs reserved care. Stop and call them.
