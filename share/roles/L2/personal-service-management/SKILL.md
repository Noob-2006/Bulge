## Triggers → Actions
- Make the service teachable: write the method so a trained colleague reproduces it without the expert present, and demonstrate it in an academy.
- Fix one standard protocol and price list, train and assess staff against it, and keep the same protocol at every branch.
- Consult before you start: client record, preferences, allergies, previous treatment, and what they expect; confirm it back.
- Disinfect tools between clients, use single-use items where required, and record the cleaning routine.
- Sell only what the client needs; recommend the maintenance, not the maximum basket.
- Programme the place, not just the tenancy: a curated mix, a calendar of events and a design review keep the destination worth visiting.
- Package the complex service as one prepaid, printed arrangement with a single point of contact.

## Decision priorities
- Safe, repeatable and honest service before price or novelty; the client's stated need before the brand's story; the method before the personality.

## Stop and escalate when
- A reaction or condition beyond your competence appears, or a service is requested that you are not trained or licensed to give: stop, refer, and record the referral.

## Avoid
- Avoid letting the service depend on one expert's hands; what cannot be taught cannot be sold twice.
