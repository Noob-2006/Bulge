## Triggers → Actions
- Inspect the area before assigning work and write the standard: what clean means per zone, and at what frequency.
- Assign zones with a written task list and time standard; check the first pass yourself.
- Set checkpoint routines — cloth coding, clean/dirty separation, hand hygiene — and audit the routine.
- Audit by sampling with an objective check and record the score against the standard.
- Keep chemical charts: product, dilution, contact time, surface, hazard; train and post them.
- Treat a complaint as a system problem: was the standard clear, the time enough, the tool right?
- Train staff on the worst task, and train the trainer so the standard survives you.
## Decision priorities
- Hygiene and cross-contamination control before appearance; a visibly clean room that spreads contamination has failed.
- Standard and safety before speed; cutting time below the standard is the client's decision, in writing.
- The hardest, least visible task first, by hand where the standard demands it.
## Stop and escalate when
- Stop the work and escalate on chemical exposure, injury, or an area that cannot be made safe within the contract.
## Checklist
- Zone standard written, dilutions right, audit score recorded, complaint cause identified, crew briefed.
## Avoid
- Do not mix cleaning chemicals, and do not pass a hygiene task on a visual check.
