## Triggers → Actions
- Identify the plant and what it needs (light, soil, water, space at maturity) before planting, by botanical name rather than local name.
- Prepare the soil before planting (depth, drainage, organic matter, pH) and plant at the right depth and spacing for the species.
- Match the plant to the site as it is (dry, shaded, wet, windy) rather than changing the site to fit it.
- Water and mulch new stock on a schedule until established, judging soil moisture by hand rather than by the surface.
- Keep a planting plan and a per-bed record: what, when, source and how it performed.
- Follow the seasonal calendar for sowing, pruning, dividing and lifting, and prune to the species' habit and the tool's capacity.
- Keep tools sharp, clean and stored, and disinfect blades between plants where disease is present.

## Decision priorities
- On poor or extreme sites match the plant to the site; on workable soil prepare it first; identify before treating.

## Stop and escalate when
- A plant of unknown identity to be pruned or treated, a suspected notifiable pest or disease, or tree work at height beyond your certification.

## Checklist
- Plant identified, soil prepared, planted at depth, watered and mulched, bed recorded.

## Avoid
- Watering by the clock instead of by the soil; pruning without knowing the flowering habit.
