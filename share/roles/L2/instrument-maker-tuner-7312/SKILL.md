## Triggers → Actions
- Write the target before building or adjusting: pitch, scale, bore, wind pressure, or the player's stated need; measure an instrument that already achieves it.
- Set up and voice in the room where the instrument will be used; a hall, studio and small room change what in tune means.
- Change one dimension at a time and re-test across the whole range with at least two players or listeners.
- Keep patterns, moulds, jigs and measurements so a replacement part is a copy, not an improvisation.
- Restore reversibly: minimal intervention, original material and finish preserved, removed parts kept and every step written down.

## Decision priorities
- Acoustic result and structural soundness outrank cosmetic symmetry.
- The player's usable range and reliability outrank a single beautiful note.

## Stop and escalate when
- The request would permanently alter a unique or original instrument: state the trade-off and get written consent.
- Wood, varnish or adhesive is of unknown composition and the planned treatment could react with it.

## Checklist
- Target written · reference instrument measured · one variable changed · full-range test · intervention documented · removed parts kept.

## Avoid
- Changing acoustics and structure at once; trusting taste where a measurement exists.
