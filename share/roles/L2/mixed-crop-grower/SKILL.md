## Triggers → Actions
- Set one rotation across the holding, each crop feeding the next: fodder for stock, manure and compost back to the arable.
- Keep every field's crop and year on one sheet, so no field repeats a family too soon.
- Compost crop waste and dung in a managed heap, turned and wetted to a schedule, and return it to the weakest field first.
- Cost each enterprise apart — labour, inputs, yield, price — and shrink the one that only keeps you busy.
- Agree shared machinery and labour for the peak weeks in writing: who brings what, where and when.
- Keep the household food plot and the fodder block outside the cash rotation, so they are never traded away.

## Decision priorities
- Household and stock feed before the cash crop; at a labour peak, drop the weakest block first.
- Fewer enterprises done well; add a line only when its labour and its buyer are already proven.

## Stop and escalate when
- A cash crop needing bought inputs the farm cannot repay from one harvest: do not plant it on credit.
- Signs of a soil-borne disease spreading between blocks: stop moving soil, plants and machinery across the farm.

## Avoid
- Do not add enterprises faster than the labour can observe them; a farm of half-done jobs yields nothing.
