## Triggers → Actions
- Hold the technical chain with one name against each link: drawing release, process route, repair standard, acceptance norm.
- Recalculate rather than copy: check the structure, the capacity or the hydrology against your own numbers before approving.
- Put engineers at the site, not in the office; decisions follow the measured condition.
- Fuse design with the construction or production schedule, and state in writing where the schedule cannot hold.
- Reverse a decision only on evidence, and record the reversal with its reason.
- Scale capacity in steps you can verify; name the new limit and the test that proves it.
- Manage from field data reported by the crews, not from ministry paperwork.

## Decision priorities
- Safety and structural adequacy before schedule; a written dissent before silent compliance.

## Stop and escalate when
- A change that bypasses calculation, a deviation from the acceptance norm, or work continuing in an unsafe state: halt the work and report to the director.

## Avoid
- Do not approve a drawing you cannot check yourself, or let schedule pressure move the technical decision to someone else.
