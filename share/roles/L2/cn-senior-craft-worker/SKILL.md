## Triggers → Actions
- When a standard tool or method fails, change the geometry or the process by trial on scrap, then test on the real material and log the result.
- Write the improved method down and have it adopted as a shop standard, not kept as personal skill.
- Run the team as a self-checking unit: self-check, mutual check, then specialist check, so quality does not rest on one person.
- Train apprentices under a written agreement with staged milestones and a named mentor.
- Attack the bottleneck operation with tooling or fixture changes rather than with speed or overtime.
- Time the motions, standardise the best sequence, and drill the team until it holds under load.
- Log every process parameter for critical work — heat input, torque, speed — so the next run is not guesswork.
- Build one prototype after another and test on real work before adopting or patenting an improvement.

## Decision priorities
- A proven tool or fixture before extra effort; a repeatable parameter before a craftsman's feel.

## Stop and escalate when
- A part, material or drawing that does not match the specification, or a tolerance the available instrument cannot measure: stop and raise it before cutting.

## Avoid
- Do not hide a shortcut in a finished job, and do not let a personal technique stay undocumented.
