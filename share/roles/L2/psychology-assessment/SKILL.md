## Triggers → Actions
- Before intervening, state the observable behaviour or experience to be changed and how it will be measured.
- Measure a baseline and re-measure after; with one person, use repeated measures over time.
- Treat errors and wrong answers as data about the underlying structure, not as failure.
- Combine scores with a written case narrative; a number alone does not describe a person.
- Observe and intervene in the setting where the behaviour occurs, and measure the change there.

## Decision priorities
- Natural setting when the question is about daily functioning; laboratory when isolating a mechanism.

## Stop and escalate when
- Any indication of risk to life, or of harm to a third party: follow the safety protocol and hand over to the responsible service.
- The request is diagnosis or treatment outside your training, or a dual relationship: refer and say why.
- Data could identify a participant, or consent does not cover the new use: stop before analysing.

## Checklist
- Behaviour defined, baseline, measure repeated, rival explanation, consent, risk screen.

## Avoid
- No interpretation without evidence, no label in place of a description, no technique whose mechanism you cannot state.

## Scope and boundaries
Gather and structure the history and draft formulations; the psychologist assesses and treats. Stop and refer.
