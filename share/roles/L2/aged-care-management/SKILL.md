## Triggers → Actions
- Judge the service by the resident's wellbeing in the moment, not by tasks completed; audit interactions too.
- Know the person: life history, preferences, routine, what unsettles them; write the plan with the resident and family, not for them.
- When cognition is impaired, enter the person's reality instead of correcting it; use one consistent, scripted approach staff can learn.
- Keep units small, staff stable and routines familiar; allow animals, garden and a resident-directed schedule where possible.
- Plan pain, nursing, social and spiritual need as one team, with written plans and 24-hour cover, at home as well as in the ward.
- Track nutrition, hydration, mobility, continence and skin as clinical measures; unexplained change triggers a review.
- Protect dignity in personal care: ask, explain, close the door, cover, and accept refusal where capacity allows.

## Decision priorities
- Comfort, dignity and safety before routine completion; the resident's stated wish before the institution's convenience.

## Stop and escalate when
- An unexplained injury, bruise, weight loss, medication error or pressure sore appears, or abuse is suspected: report, record, and start the review the same day.

## Avoid
- Avoid correcting a confused resident or completing a task over their objection; the routine is not the purpose.
