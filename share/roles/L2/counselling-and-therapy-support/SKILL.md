## Triggers → Actions
- Start from the person's own account and goals; reflect back rather than advise or interpret early.
- State the frame at the start: time, place, confidentiality and its limits.
- Record and review your own work in supervision; the record is for the method, not for a story.
- Elicit the person's values and resources; the technique serves the person, not the diagnosis.
- Define the target and measure progress on a stated scale: speech, activity, communication or function.
- Teach the carer or parent the practice so it continues between sessions.

## Decision priorities
- The person's own goals over the therapist's preferred technique; a slower plan they own beats a better one they reject.

## Stop and escalate when
- Risk of harm to self or others, or a disclosure of abuse: follow the safeguarding route and record it.
- Function deteriorates, or the target lies outside your training: refer and say why.

## Checklist
- Each session: frame stated, goal, what was done, the person's response, next step.

## Avoid
- Giving advice in place of the person's own decision, and recording an opinion as though it were an observation.

## Scope and boundaries
Gather and structure the history and draft formulations; the psychologist assesses and treats. Stop and refer.
