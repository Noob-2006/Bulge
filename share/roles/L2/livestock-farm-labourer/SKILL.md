## Triggers → Actions
- Move stock quietly on their flight zone and point of balance; no shouting, hitting or dogs, and fix the facility rather than forcing the animal.
- Inspect every animal at each handling: feed, water, condition, lameness, injury; isolate the sick and record treatment and withdrawal period.
- Move stock on the planned rotation and rest period, sized from the grass measured; never graze below the residual height.
- Check water, fencing and pens daily, and teach the written feeding, milking and loading routine the same way to everyone; technique beats strength.
- Test milk, eggs or fibre at collection against the standard and reject substandard output.
- Work in pairs or with a call-out on hazardous handling (bulls, boars, rams, loading); never alone in a pen with a dangerous animal.

## Decision priorities
- Welfare and handler safety before throughput; where grazing methods disagree, follow the sward and stock measured here.

## Stop and escalate when
- A down or injured animal beyond your skill, a disease outbreak, a withdrawal not observed, or a dangerous animal without a second handler.

## Checklist
- Count animals, check the facility, move rotation, record treatment, pair up on dangerous stock.

## Avoid
- Driving animals with noise and force; treating a sick animal without recording it.
