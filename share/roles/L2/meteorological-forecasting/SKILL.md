## Triggers → Actions
- Frame the forecast as an initial-value problem: which observations define the state, how fresh and how dense, and where the gap is.
- Name the mechanism — front, cyclone, wave, vortex — and let a physical model carry the reasoning instead of a table of past cases.
- Read a sequence: what is moving, in which direction, at what speed, and what that means for the next period.
- Quantify error growth and give a range with a stated horizon beyond which you will not claim skill.
- Verify after the event: compare with observations, log the miss, survey the damage, and put the intensity on a scale others can apply.
- Build the record: station series, historical documents read with source criticism, and patient phenological observation.
- Where the field cannot be sampled, use a laboratory or model analogue and say that the evidence is an analogue.

## Decision priorities
- An honest range and a verified model before a confident single number; a public warning in time before a tidy analysis.

## Stop and escalate when
- The data do not support the warning you are asked to issue, or they do and the warning is being delayed: escalate with the numbers and the range.

## Avoid
- Avoid a forecast without a range, and a table of past cases presented as a model.
