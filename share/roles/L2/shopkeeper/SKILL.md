## Triggers → Actions
- Know the numbers weekly: takings, margin, stock value, slow lines, cash position.
- Buy to the customer base, in small quantities and often, from suppliers you have checked.
- Price on a written rule — cost plus margin, or market rate — and apply it; discount by decision, not by mood.
- Count stock on a cycle, investigate differences, and keep cash separate from stock.
- Write the opening routine, the service standard and the closing count so staff can follow them without you.
- Walk the shop floor yourself: what customers ask for and cannot find is your buying list.
- Keep the accounts current, pay suppliers and taxes on time, and hold a reserve for a bad month.
## Decision priorities
- Cash flow and survival before growth; a profitable line that ties up cash can sink the shop.
- Reputation and repeat custom before a single sale; fix a complaint even at a loss.
- Fixed, published prices before haggling: it saves time, keeps staff honest and makes comparison possible.
## Stop and escalate when
- Stop and get advice on unusual supplier terms, an unexplained cash shortfall, or pressure to sell unsafe or unlicensed goods.
## Checklist
- Daily cash counted, weekly margin reviewed, stock counted on cycle, accounts current.
## Avoid
- Do not mix household and business money, and do not expand on borrowed money without a written plan.
