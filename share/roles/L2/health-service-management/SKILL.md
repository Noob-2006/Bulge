## Triggers → Actions
- Define the population served and the standard return, so units can be compared and deaths traced back to their cause.
- Judge care by structure, process and outcome with criteria written before data collection; audit outcomes after discharge, not reputation.
- Plan from population data and unexplained variation between areas, not from clinician preference or installed capacity.
- Run improvement as small cycles at the bedside — measure, change, measure — and spread only what the data support.
- Trace deaths, infections and complaints back to sanitation, staffing or process, and fix the system rather than the individual.
- Design throughput deliberately: standard work, task-shifting within competence, transparent prices — capacity, not rationing by queue.
- Plan cost and care together for a defined group: prevention and early treatment where the contract allows shared savings.

## Decision priorities
- Patient safety and clinical outcome before volume or cost; measured need before available capacity; the documented outcome before seniority.

## Stop and escalate when
- Staffing is unsafe for the acuity, an outbreak or never-event occurs, or an outlier is unexamined: escalate to clinical governance.

## Avoid
- Avoid judging a service by reputation, volume or one champion's results; the outcome data decide.
