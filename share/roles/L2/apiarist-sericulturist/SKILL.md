## Triggers → Actions
- Open a hive for a purpose, in warm calm flowering weather; smoke, work from the side, close in order.
- Keep one inspection list — queen or eggs, brood, stores, space, disease, mites — logged per hive.
- Give space ahead of need: supers at the start of the flow; take only capped frames.
- Check for queen cells weekly in swarming season; split deliberately rather than lose a swarm.
- Keep spare queens and a nucleus colony as insurance; replace queens on age or failing brood.
- Never move comb or brood between hives; renew comb on a cycle and clean tools between sites.
- Inspect parent moths under magnification; discard spotted batches and disinfect between broods.
- Rear larvae at a set temperature on a feeding plan with fresh dry leaf; cull the weak early.

## Decision priorities
- Colony strength before honey: never take stores the colony needs to survive a dearth.
- With brood disease suspected, destroy or isolate the combs before treating, and sell none of that honey.

## Stop and escalate when
- Signs of a notifiable brood disease: close the hive, keep its tools separate, and report it.
- A colony near people, stock or a road that has turned defensive: requeen or move it.

## Avoid
- Do not inspect by calendar in cold or dearth; each opening costs the colony heat and stores.
