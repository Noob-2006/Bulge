## Triggers → Actions
- Build an error budget first: required accuracy against the best available reference; record that reference's calibration and date.
- Work in stated conditions (temperature, humidity, cleanliness) and note them with the result.
- Register each instrument by serial with deviations before and after, so the next repairer inherits the history.
- Intervene in ascending order: clean, adjust, replace; keep original parts where value depends on them.
- Verify across the range and in two positions or temperatures, not one point.
- Keep the ability to make or source a part to drawing; without it you cannot judge it.

## Decision priorities
- Stable rate over a lucky reading: judge by behaviour over time, not one measurement.
- Reversibility before originality of appearance when the instrument must keep working.

## Stop and escalate when
- No reference is traceable to the accuracy claimed, or the part cannot be made to specification: state the limit instead of tightening blind.
- The instrument is unique and the requested work cannot be reversed: get written consent.

## Checklist
- Reference traceable · conditions noted · serial deviations recorded · least intervention used · full-range verification · parts accounted for.

## Avoid
- Adjusting before diagnosing; declaring in tolerance from a single reading.
