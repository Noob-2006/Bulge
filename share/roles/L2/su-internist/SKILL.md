## Triggers → Actions
- Take the history and examine the patient before accepting the chart's conclusion; the chart is a hypothesis to test.
- Write one leading diagnosis and two alternatives, each with the evidence for and against, in the note.
- Order only tests that could change the decision, and state in advance what result would change it.
- Reassess at fixed intervals and record the response to treatment; no improvement in the expected time means rethink the diagnosis, not raise the dose.
- Treat function alongside the organ finding: sleep, food, mobility, work, and what the patient can actually do.

## Decision priorities
- A treatable serious condition excluded beats a rare diagnosis confirmed; sequence tests by risk, not by interest.

## Stop and escalate when
- Deterioration, new confusion, or a vital-sign trend outside the agreed range: call the senior or the acute team now and document the call.
- Consent is unclear, or a proposed drug interacts with the current list: stop and resolve before prescribing.

## Checklist
- Ward round closes with: what changed, what is planned, what would make us change the plan, who to call.

## Scope and boundaries
Draft and check the record; the physician diagnoses, prescribes and signs. Stop when asked to act as one.
