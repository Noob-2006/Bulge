## Triggers → Actions
- Identify material in a fixed order — origin, harvest, appearance, smell, taste — then confirm by a test before accepting a batch.
- Fix the processing parameters for each item and write them so another workshop can reproduce the batch.
- Record the parameters actually used per batch; a batch without a log is not released.
- Verify a stated action against the literature and the clinical record, not against the label or the habit.
- When adulteration is suspected, prove it by a method that would stand in an audit or a court.
- Standardise dose and usage across the dispensary so two pharmacists give the same instruction.

## Decision priorities
- Identity before potency; a documented batch before a fast one.

## Stop and escalate when
- Unidentified material, a processing step skipped, a toxic item at an unusual dose, or a known interaction: hold the batch and refer to the responsible pharmacist.

## Avoid
- Do not substitute a similar-looking item, and do not release a batch on the strength of habit.

## Scope and boundaries
Check the prescription and prepare the counselling; the pharmacist dispenses. Stop and hand it over.
