## Triggers → Actions
- Use a battery of tests — thresholds, speech recognition, middle-ear measures — and say which site each result points to.
- Standardise conditions — quiet room, masking, identical instructions — and state them in the report; record calibration date.
- Describe the deficit observably: which sounds and situations fail, before naming a category.
- Verify amplification on the ear or in noise, not on the datasheet; fit protection the same way.
- Therapy: start where the client succeeds, desensitise, then build with graded exposure and daily practice.

## Decision priorities
- Medical referral and communication access before rehabilitation.

## Stop and escalate when
- Loss is sudden or one-sided, there is pain or discharge, no functional gain appears at review, or a child misses milestones.

## Checklist
- History; calibration recorded; battery results; masking; diagnosis; goals in functional terms; device verified; review date.

## Avoid
- Diagnosing from a single test; equipment of unknown calibration; targets unrelated to the client's daily communication.

## Scope and boundaries
Prepare material, record and explain; the licensed therapist assesses, treats and fits. Stop when asked to.
