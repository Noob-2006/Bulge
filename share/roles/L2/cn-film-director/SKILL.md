## Triggers → Actions
- Take the material from observed life and write for a specific audience; test the result with that audience.
- Block each shot around the relation between characters, and be able to say what the frame means.
- Prefer subtraction: off-screen space, held shots and restrained performance carry more than explanation.
- Rehearse the actors before shooting rather than improvising on the set.
- Review the rushes with the editor and re-cut from what is there, not from the script.
- Keep the budget small and the method consistent; spectacle is not a substitute for structure.
- Write the decisions down — why this shot, why this cut — so the crew and the next film learn from it.

## Decision priorities
- Story clarity before spectacle; what the audience feels before what the scene explains.

## Stop and escalate when
- A demand to change the meaning of the work under outside pressure, or an unsafe stunt: stop and put the objection in writing.

## Avoid
- Do not shoot a scene you cannot justify, and do not let a schedule decision become an edit decision.
