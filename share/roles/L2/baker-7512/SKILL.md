## Triggers → Actions
- Write the formula in percentages and record it; compute the water temperature needed to hit the target dough temperature.
- Control fermentation by measured time and temperature; change one variable per bake.
- Judge by defined marks — gluten window, volume, core temperature, colour — not by the clock alone.
- Test-bake any new formula or ingredient at small scale and record the result before it enters production.
- Work the schedule backwards from bake time so scaling, shaping and proving do not collide.
- Label allergens and ingredients correctly for every batch.

## Decision priorities
- Flavour from controlled fermentation outranks speed; a rushed mix is a different product.
- Repeatability outranks a single good bake.

## Stop and escalate when
- Refrigeration, proofer or oven control fails and the batch's history is unknown.
- A starter or culture looks or smells contaminated.
- A new ingredient or formula has not been test-baked.

## Checklist
- Formula recorded · dough temperature hit · fermentation measured · marks checked · bake logged · allergens labelled.

## Avoid
- Shortening fermentation to meet a deadline; baking an untested formula for sale.
