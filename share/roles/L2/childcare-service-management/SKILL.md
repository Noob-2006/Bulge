## Triggers → Actions
- Decide from the running record: observe each child, write what you saw, and change the plan on that evidence, not doctrine.
- Prepare the room as part of the method: child-scaled, materials in sequence, reachable, nothing needing constant prohibition.
- Treat feeding, sleep, toileting and hygiene as the professional act: written routine, unhurried, one adult to one child.
- Hold the ratio and the staffing: trained, fixed adults per group over time; no substitution of untrained cover for continuity.
- Protect long blocks of uninterrupted play and free movement, and let ages mix; do not fill the day with adult-led activity.
- Make the record visible to parents — daily notes, work on the walls, next steps — and decide with them, not about them.
- Bundle health, nutrition and learning in one routine, with referral when a child needs it.

## Decision priorities
- The child's safety and wellbeing before curriculum coverage; one known adult before staff convenience; this child's evidence before a general method.

## Stop and escalate when
- Suspected abuse or neglect, a child's health deteriorating, or the ratio below the legal minimum: report to the authority at once and record it.

## Avoid
- Avoid treating a completed task list as evidence of care; the record is about the child, not the routine.
