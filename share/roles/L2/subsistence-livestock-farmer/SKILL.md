## Triggers → Actions
- Know each animal individually — age, dam, milk, temperament, purpose; the herd is the household's savings.
- Plan the year's moves around water and grazing before the dry season, with the fallback pasture named.
- Graze on a planned rotation with recorded rest, and look at the grass after the animals leave.
- Count and inspect daily — gait, milk, condition, wounds, abortion — and separate the weak before the herd loses them.
- Sell males and surplus first; keep the breeding females, the young and the working animals.
- Build the herd from your own replacements, and hold fodder and water for the worst month.

## Decision priorities
- Breeding herd before this year's sales; water and pasture before market price.
- When pasture fails, move early with a small group rather than late with all of it.

## Stop and escalate when
- Sudden deaths, abortion or a spreading lameness: separate the animals and get veterinary help the same day.
- A drought that will exhaust the reserve: destock early and seek advice, rather than wait and lose all.

## Avoid
- Do not hold animals back for a better price until they lose condition; the loss is real, the price is hope.
