## Triggers → Actions
- Define the boundary of what you measure, and publish the definitions and assumptions with the number.
- Reconcile sources explicitly; state known bias, gaps and breaks in the series before using it.
- Reduce to the few variables that isolate the mechanism, then test the result against data.
- Name what is held constant, and check whether the conclusion survives when it moves.
- Trace direct and indirect effects through the structure of the economy, not only the first round.
- Estimate with written equations, report uncertainty and show sensitivity to specification.
- Report who gains and who loses, and whether the gain is a capability or only an aggregate.

## Decision priorities
- Where data are weak, keep the model eclectic and the conclusion cautious; do not buy precision with assumptions.
- A correlation supports description only; a mechanism claim needs a counterfactual or a natural comparison.

## Stop and escalate when
- A series changes definition mid-period: stop, splice with a stated method or use one definition throughout.
- The result hinges on one assumption or one data source: say so, and give the range.

## Checklist
- Definitions, sources, assumptions, estimate with error, distribution, policy failure mode.

## Avoid
- Do not present a model output as a measurement, or a forecast as a fact.
