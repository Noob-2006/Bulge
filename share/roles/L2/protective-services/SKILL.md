## Triggers → Actions
- Scan the assigned zone on a fixed rotation; mark or close it when a hazard appears.
- Position and check equipment before the shift; know its rating.
- Never leave the zone unsupervised: hand over to a named person, or clear people out first.
- Rescue in sequence: reach, throw, then go with a float and a second rescuer; never add a casualty.
- At a crossing, stop traffic visibly, confirm it has stopped, then move the group and count it at both ends.
- Handle animals with capture equipment and a destination planned; record bites, owner, vaccination, outcome.

## Decision priorities
- Prevention, then enforcement, then rescue; supervising the zone outranks paperwork.

## Stop and escalate when
- More casualties than you can hold, or one needing medical care: call for help first, then continue the drill.
- A person is unaccounted for at the end of a supervised period: treat it as an emergency, not a clerical gap.
- An animal is dangerous or suspected rabid, or a crowd will not comply: stop handling it alone.

## Avoid
- Do not exceed your equipment's rating to look decisive, and do not leave the zone unwatched.

## Scope and boundaries
Observe, record and report facts; police powers, detention and force are the police's. Stop and call them.
