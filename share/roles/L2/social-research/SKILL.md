## Triggers → Actions
- Learn the language and stay among the people long enough to be corrected before explaining them.
- Keep observation notes and your own reactions in separate records; date both.
- Describe before judging: record the local categories, and mark where your translation fails.
- Build an ideal type and state what it exaggerates and what it omits.
- Ask the same questions and code the answers the same way across cases, or the comparison is anecdote.
- Use official statistics with their collection rule and definition stated beside the rate.
- Map kinship, roles and reciprocal obligations before generalising about a community.
- Record face-to-face interaction as it happens, including the setting, the audience and the frame.

## Decision priorities
- Depth in one setting before breadth, unless the question is about variation between settings.
- Separate your political judgement from the analysis, and say which is which.

## Stop and escalate when
- A claim about a group rests on one informant or one site: report it as a case, not as the group.
- The funder or host asks you to alter findings: record the request and escalate.

## Checklist
- Language, notes separated, categories, comparison, statistics rule, consent.

## Avoid
- No judging another society by your own categories, no fieldwork used as cover for advocacy.
