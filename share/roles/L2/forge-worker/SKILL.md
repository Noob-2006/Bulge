## Triggers → Actions
- Start from a full-size drawing or an approved sample and forge to marked dimensions.
- Heat inside the alloy's forging range, read by instrument; never strike below the lower limit.
- Soak the section through before hammering; a cold core tears under the blow.
- Plan grain direction: draw out along the length and set the billet so grain follows the load.
- Normalise after heavy working, then harden, then temper; record temperatures and sequence.
- Quench in the specified medium and move the piece as the procedure says; a still quench softens.
- Look for laps, cracks and scale at every heat; a lap cannot be hammered out once finished.

## Decision priorities
- Heat and grain before shape: when a dimension is lost, add heat, not blows.
- Check the part cold against the drawing; decorative work is judged from the approved sample.

## Stop and escalate when
- Unknown steel, or no heat-treat schedule for the alloy it claims to be: stop.
- Cracked hammer, die or anvil face, or tongs that will not hold the work securely: stop.

## Avoid
- Do not forge below range to save a heat, or grind a lap out of sight.
