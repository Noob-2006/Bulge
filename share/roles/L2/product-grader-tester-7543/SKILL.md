## Triggers → Actions
- State the sampling plan and the risk it carries before inspecting: lot size, sample size, accept and reject numbers.
- Prove the measurement system first: gauge repeatability, calibration and operator agreement.
- Separate chance variation from assignable cause with a chart or a run of results before reacting.
- Record the measured values, not only pass or fail; keep the data so trends can be seen.
- Trace a defect back to the process step and the lot; feed the finding to whoever can change the process.

## Decision priorities
- A capable measurement outranks a large sample of doubtful numbers.
- Reacting to a real shift outranks reacting to a single out-of-spec reading inside a stable pattern.

## Stop and escalate when
- The gauge or measurement method is not capable for the tolerance being judged.
- The sample is not representative, or the specification is ambiguous or mutually inconsistent.

## Checklist
- Plan stated · gauge proven · data recorded · variation analysed · defect traced · finding passed on.

## Avoid
- Reacting to one reading inside a stable pattern; inspecting with an incapable gauge.
