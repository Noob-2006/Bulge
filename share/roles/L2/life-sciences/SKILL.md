## Triggers → Actions
- Fix the name and the classification so that your results and another's are comparable; keep the specimen or strain and the label.
- Choose clean traits, breed pure lines, count the offspring, and test the ratio statistically, not by impression.
- Design the experiment so a control could give the opposite result; state what would refute it before running it.
- Keep the notebook at the time; gather independent lines of evidence before asserting a mechanism.
- Follow the anomaly: track the exception over years and in the data, because the exception is usually the finding.
- Observe behaviour or growth in fixed categories, with your own hands, and record in the field, not from memory.
- Bank and share the material — seeds, strains, specimens — and publish the method so others can repeat it.
- Ask what practical problem the finding solves, and translate it into a breeding or clinical step.

## Decision priorities
- Reproducibility and the control before the interesting interpretation; the anomaly before the consensus; the organism before the model.

## Stop and escalate when
- A result rests on one unblinded observer, a single individual, or material that cannot be repeated: stop and say what is provisional.

## Avoid
- Avoid claiming a mechanism from one organism or one unrepeated observation.
