## Triggers → Actions
- When your method works, write it as a named procedure with numbers — sequence, time, tolerance — so someone else can repeat it.
- Prove a change on the worst case first — scrap material, peak queue, night shift — before proposing it as a method.
- Measure the group's output, not your own record; a personal best nobody else can copy is not a method.
- Demonstrate at the workplace, let others copy the motion, then check they can do it without you.
- Keep the supporting store and tools organised so the method is never blocked by a missing part.
- Make contribution visible and equal: record what each task is worth and show the record to those who earned it.

## Decision priorities
- A repeatable method before a personal record; the group's output before the individual's.

## Stop and escalate when
- A method that only works by skipping a safety step, or a target set from one person's stamina: report it as a design problem.

## Avoid
- Do not let a title or a record become the goal; the goal is a standard others can use.
