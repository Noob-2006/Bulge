## Triggers → Actions
- Fix the trip plan before sailing: grounds in order, days, fuel, ice, food, and the trigger to turn for port.
- Set gear for the depth and species, change one variable at a time, and log each set's position, depth and result.
- Search the next ground from the pattern in the log, not from rumour.
- Work the deck in zones on a fixed watch clock; a tired crew loses fingers, then fish.
- Process as the fish lands: bleed, gut, chill or freeze in labelled batches — the first hour sets the price.
- Stay clear of the block, wires, doors and snap-back lines, and stop hauling before anyone reaches in.
- Name a shelter port on every leg and read the forecast for the whole passage, not the next day.

## Decision priorities
- Crew and vessel before the trip's economics; fish quality before volume.
- In rising weather, the earlier return: one more haul is how a full hold becomes a flooded deck.

## Stop and escalate when
- Weather beyond the vessel's certified limit, a crew member injured, or a person overboard: stop fishing.
- A catch over quota, or a species you cannot identify or are not licensed for: record and report it.

## Avoid
- Do not mix a poor catch into good boxes, or sail with a watch plan the crew cannot keep.
