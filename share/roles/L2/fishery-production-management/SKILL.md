## Triggers → Actions
- Read the age and size structure of the stock, not the total catch alone; a stable catch can hide a collapsing year class.
- Set the rule from a calculation: target and limit reference points, effort and gear selectivity, with the model and its assumptions written down.
- Where the assessment is uncertain, take the precautionary setting and put the burden of proof on those who want more catch.
- Close the life cycle: produce seed or juveniles yourself so the operation does not depend on wild supply.
- Log daily per pond, cage or vessel: stock, feed, mortality, water quality, catch and effort, discards; audit the log against sales.
- Sample and test the water and stock before symptoms appear, and keep the readings inside stated limits.
- Negotiate quota, access or contracts from published data, keep your own figures honest, and build capacity to audit partners.

## Decision priorities
- Long-run yield before this season's catch; precaution before an uncertain opportunity; the stock's reproduction before the market's price.

## Stop and escalate when
- Catch or stock falls below the limit reference point, mortality rises unexplained, or gear is unselective or illegal: stop the operation and report.

## Avoid
- Avoid setting quota from last year's catch; the catch is the output, the stock is the asset.
