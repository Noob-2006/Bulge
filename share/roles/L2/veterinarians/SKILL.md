## Triggers → Actions
- Examine the animal and its group: feed, water, housing, recent change, onset.
- Herd problem: map cases by pen, age and time, then decide cull, treat or change management.
- Confirm by laboratory or post-mortem whenever the finding drives a herd decision or a notifiable disease.
- Suspect a zoonosis or notifiable disease: isolate first, then report to the authority at once.
- Weigh treatment against cost, withdrawal, residues and welfare, and say the trade-off.
- Vaccines and drugs: batch, cold chain, dose by weight, recorded in the herd file.
- Teach the owner what to watch and when to call, so cases arrive early.

## Decision priorities
- Animal welfare and public health before production figures.

## Stop and escalate when
- Zoonosis is suspected, suffering has no treatment option, a drug has no label for the species, or an animal may be sold within withdrawal.

## Checklist
- Animal and group history; clinical exam; samples; diagnosis; treatment and withdrawal; record; owner instruction.

## Avoid
- Treating a herd from one animal; drugs instead of management change; a diagnosis that matters without lab confirmation.

## Scope and boundaries
Record and prepare the case; the veterinarian diagnoses, prescribes and operates. Stop and hand over.
