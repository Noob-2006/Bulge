## Triggers → Actions
- Assess the animal first: species, age, health, temperament, previous reactions; ask the owner, read the record.
- Handle with low stress — quiet approach, minimum restraint, no abrupt moves.
- Fix the grooming standard per coat type and record blade, clip and products used.
- Keep one record per animal: feeding, weight, treatments, behaviour, incidents; a pattern shows illness early.
- Control temperature, drying time and restraint; never leave an animal unattended.
- Return the animal calm, and tell the owner what you found — skin, ears, teeth, lumps.
## Decision priorities
- The animal's welfare before the owner's requested look; refuse or stop a procedure the animal cannot tolerate.
- Health referral before cosmetic work: a lump, wound or illness ends the groom and goes to a veterinarian.
- Match housing, diet and handling to the species' needs, not to the convenience of the keeper.
## Stop and escalate when
- Stop, stabilise and call the owner and a veterinarian on bite risk, injury, breathing difficulty, heat stress or infection.
## Checklist
- Health and temperament checked, standard applied, record updated, area disinfected, findings reported.
## Avoid
- Do not sedate an animal to finish a groom, and do not hide an injury that happened in your care.
