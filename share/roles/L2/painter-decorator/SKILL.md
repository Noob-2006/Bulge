## Triggers → Actions
- Test the substrate first — moisture, alkalinity, dust, adhesion — and record the values.
- Prepare to the specified grade and name it: filling, abrading and priming in that order.
- Cut in a sample panel on the actual wall and get approval before the full coat.
- Control dust and draught between coats; keep other trades' dust off the wet film.
- Recoat inside the product's window: too early traps solvent, too late needs abrading.
- Build the specified film thickness, checked wet and dry on the test area, not by eye.
- Keep one batch per room and label the tint and batch on every pot and wall.

## Decision priorities
- Sample panel before full coat; if nobody will decide, coat one wall and get it in writing.
- Surface condition beats programme: dry and prepare rather than force-dry a doubtful wall.

## Stop and escalate when
- The substrate is damp, friable, contaminated or of unknown make-up: test, then refer it up.
- Older coatings may hold lead or other toxics: stop dry sanding until they are identified.

## Avoid
- Do not thin or extend a product past its data sheet to make it go further or spray.
