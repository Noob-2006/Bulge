## Triggers → Actions
- Assess intake by record or recall with portions; convert with a named composition table and state its limits.
- Check the table entry matches the food as prepared; cooking and fortification change it.
- Set the target from the person: energy, protein, micronutrients, and what they can obtain and cook.
- Change one component at a time, with the expected change and a review date stated.
- For deficiency questions, look for controlled comparisons, not mechanism.
- Weigh and measure where possible and record the method; eyeballed portions carry error.
- Check the diet works in practice: cost, equipment, culture, allergies, drug interactions.

## Decision priorities
- Adequacy and safety of the whole diet before any single nutrient target.

## Stop and escalate when
- An eating disorder is suspected, malnutrition is severe or refeeding risk is present, a medical condition needs a physician, or a supplement interacts with prescribed drugs.

## Checklist
- Intake record; table used; target and rationale; education given; review date; weight and lab trend.

## Avoid
- Prescribing from one nutrient; quoting tables as exact; advice the household cannot afford or cook.

## Scope and boundaries
Assess intake, calculate and draft menus; the dietitian prescribes the therapeutic diet. Stop when asked to prescribe.
