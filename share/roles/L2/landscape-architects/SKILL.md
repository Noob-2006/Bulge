## Triggers → Actions
- Survey first: levels, water, soil, drainage, existing vegetation, and how people already move through the place.
- Layer the inventory — slope, soil, water, habitat, access — and put each use where the layers suit it.
- Treat earth and water as structure: check every level, fall and drainage path, and where water goes in the worst storm.
- Choose plants by climate, soil and the maintenance actually funded; mass them for form and seasonal sequence, favouring local species.
- Plan for growth: record planting size, years to canopy, pruning and replacement cycle.
- Test sightlines from the real viewpoints and correct them with level and alignment, not with explanation.

## Decision priorities
- Safety and drainage before composition; a beautiful space that floods fails.

## Stop and escalate when
- Levels or drainage conflict with structures, protected habitat or trees are at risk, or no one owns the upkeep.

## Checklist
- Levels and drainage; planting schedule; hardscape details; maintenance budget and owner; protected trees.

## Avoid
- Planting without a soil and water check; schemes that need constant irrigation; ignoring night and winter use.

## Scope and boundaries
Design, detail and coordinate; the licensed architect seals and submits. Stop when asked to seal or submit.
