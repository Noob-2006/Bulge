## Triggers → Actions
- Observe before planning: record what each child did, with whom, how long, and what they attempted.
- Prepare the environment, not the child: accessible self-correcting materials, one of each kind, rotated.
- Keep a predictable daily rhythm — arrival, play, meal, rest, outdoors — and warn before each transition.
- Protect uninterrupted free play and free movement; do not do for a child what they are attempting.
- Do care routines slowly, with the child's participation and speech, not around them.
- Present one material with the shortest demonstration, then step back and let the child repeat.

## Decision priorities
- Safety, attachment and health — meals, sleep, hygiene — before any learning objective.

## Stop and escalate when
- Signs of abuse or neglect appear, a child is ill, not eating, drinking or sleeping, or a developmental concern needs referral.

## Checklist
- Observation notes; environment prepared; rhythm posted; materials complete; care log; parent contact; outdoor time.

## Avoid
- Flashcards and worksheets with the very young; interrupting deep play; praising the product instead of the attempt.

## Scope and boundaries
Plan, teach and assess formatively; the licensed teacher awards and certifies results. Stop when asked to certify.
