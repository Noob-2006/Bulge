## Triggers → Actions
- Write the standard as working traits first — what the animal must do — and score candidates under real work, not in a ring.
- Keep a studbook per individual: parentage, health screens, temperament, working test, and the reason for every exclusion.
- For a wild species, study it in the field first — season, diet, shelter, breeding, stress signs — before building pens.
- Keep training in the same programme as breeding; sell the trained animal, not the raw one.
- Screen for inherited defects and stop a line as soon as a defect repeats.
- Hold a market line only after the working stock is secure; a type that cannot do the job is a dead end.

## Decision priorities
- Welfare and function before appearance; a line that fails in temperament leaves the programme even at a price.
- Small, recorded numbers before volume: a proven line is worth more than a large unpedigreed one.

## Stop and escalate when
- A species needing a permit, licence or health certificate, or one with an escape risk: do not proceed without it.
- An animal needing treatment beyond your competence, or showing a sign that can pass to people: isolate, get a veterinarian.

## Avoid
- Do not breed for sale before the temperament and health base is proven; the market returns the defect.
