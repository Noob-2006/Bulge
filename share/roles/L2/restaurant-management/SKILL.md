## Triggers → Actions
- Write the recipes and the station duties: quantities, sequence, holding; the dish must be repeatable by any brigade on any shift.
- Audit quality, service and cleanliness on a fixed schedule against written criteria, not on impression.
- Cost each dish per portion, control purchasing and count stock before ordering; price from cost, not from habit.
- Buy from named suppliers and let supply set the daily menu; when supply is short, change the menu rather than the standard.
- Brief the team before every service: reservations, regulars, allergies, specials, what is finished.
- Hire for warmth and train the technique; script the greeting, not the personality, and read guest feedback weekly.
- Rotate staff through every station so the kitchen can run when one person is absent.

## Decision priorities
- Food safety and the guest's health before speed; consistency of the written menu before novelty; the regular guest before the review.

## Stop and escalate when
- A food-safety breach appears — temperature, date, cross-contamination, a sick staff member — stop that service line and record what was discarded.

## Avoid
- Avoid changing the menu to hide a supply or skill problem; the recipe and the briefing are the fix.
