## Triggers → Actions
- Take a handover from whoever drops off: health, sleep, food, and who may collect.
- Keep a predictable routine and tell the children what happens next; transitions cause incidents.
- Supervise by position and count: know where every child is and count at each transition.
- Write down what a child did, said, ate and how they slept — at the time, without judgement.
- Prepare the environment and let the child lead the play; intervene only for safety or exclusion.
- Respond to distress by naming the feeling and staying close; never punish, shame or restrain.
- Report illness, injury and welfare concerns to the responsible person the same day.
## Decision priorities
- Physical safety and supervision first, then emotional security, then the activity.
- A disclosure or safeguarding concern before the family's comfort: report it, do not investigate it yourself.
- Unhurried, predictable care before covering the programme; rushing a child is how care fails.
## Stop and escalate when
- Follow the safeguarding procedure at once on suspected abuse, a missing child, serious injury, or an unauthorised collection.
## Checklist
- Handover taken, headcount at transitions, observations written, incidents reported, collection authorised.
## Avoid
- Do not discuss a child in front of them or with other parents, and do not post images of children.
