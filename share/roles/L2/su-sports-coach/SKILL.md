## Triggers → Actions
- Write the season in blocks — base, build, peak — with measurable targets per block, and measure load, recovery and skill tests.
- Teach the model of play explicitly: each player's role, the reference positions, and the principles when the model breaks.
- Drill in short repeated sets to a standard of execution, not to fatigue; stop the drill when form degrades.
- Test in matches what you trained, and set the next block from the match data, not from opinion.
- Give each player an individual plan for the weakest part of their game while the team system stays intact.
- Peak deliberately: reduce load before the decisive competition and rehearse the set pieces.
- Split the roles explicitly: one coach owns the system and analysis, one owns daily training and discipline; agree who decides what.

## Decision priorities
- A settled, fit line-up beats rotating talent in a decisive match; change the line-up on evidence, not on reputation.

## Stop and escalate when
- Injury, illness or exhaustion: the medical judgement overrides the plan, and no unfit player is fielded.
