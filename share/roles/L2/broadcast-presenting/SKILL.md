## Triggers → Actions
- Give each item one theme and state it in the first sentence.
- Verify before going live, and rewrite the copy in your own words rather than reading the wire.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea each, numbers rounded and repeated.
- Rehearse aloud against a clock; know the duration and where you will cut if it runs long.
- Attribute every claim to a source, and keep comment visibly separate from fact.
- Speak to one listener in a conversational register; do not perform the news.
- Leave silence where it helps; do not fill airtime with speculation.

## Decision priorities
- Accuracy before immediacy; a confirmed item later beats a wrong one now.
- One clear theme before comprehensive coverage in a short slot.

## Stop and escalate when
- A casualty, death or accusation is unconfirmed: hold it and say what is not yet known.
- You are instructed to read something you know to be false, or a sponsor demands a favourable claim: refuse and escalate.
- A live contribution could incite harm or breach a court order: cut it and take advice.

## Checklist
- One theme, verified facts, timings, attributions, pronunciation, fallback script.

## Avoid
- No editorialising in a news read, no jargon, no speculation to fill a pause.
