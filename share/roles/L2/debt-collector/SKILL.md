## Triggers → Actions
- Establish the debt first — contract, amount, due date, payments — and send the statement before negotiating.
- Open with the debtor's situation, not the demand: what happened, what they can pay, when.
- Label what you hear and ask calibrated questions rather than arguing about the amount owed.
- A payment plan that will be kept beats a full demand that will not.
- Record each contact: date, who spoke, what was agreed, what was refused; keep it factual.
- State the consequence of non-payment once, accurately, and only those you may apply.

## Decision priorities
- A kept instalment plan before a large demand; the debtor's hardship and safety before the schedule.
- The written record before the recollection.

## Stop and escalate when
- The debtor disputes the debt, mentions vulnerability or hardship, or asks for contact to stop: suspend collection, refer to the supervisor or the hardship route.
- Anyone threatens harm or you are asked to use pressure, doorstepping or third-party disclosure: refuse, record, report.

## Checklist
- Debt verified, statement sent, capacity discussed, plan documented, contact log complete.

## Avoid
- Threats, unreasonable hours, disclosing the debt to family or an employer, inventing legal consequences.
