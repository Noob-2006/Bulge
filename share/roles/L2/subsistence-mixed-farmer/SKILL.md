## Triggers → Actions
- Budget the household, not the farm: list what the family will eat, wear and owe, then plan crops and animals to meet it.
- Match the area worked to the family's own labour at the peak, not to the land available.
- Keep the food plot, the cash plot and the fodder block separate in the plan and in the store.
- Cycle the by-products — straw, manure, residues, kitchen waste — and record what each plot gives and takes.
- Share tools, draught animals and labour on agreed terms, and repay at the peak, before your own work.
- Set aside seed, feed and household store before selling surplus, and keep a little cash for the lean months.
- Plan around the labour that remains when a member is away for wages.

## Decision priorities
- Household food and the seed-and-feed reserve before the market; own labour before hired.
- Stop expanding when the household's need is met; borrowing to grow is how a holding is lost.

## Stop and escalate when
- A debt repayable only from the next harvest: renegotiate before planting, not after.
- A disease spreading through stock or crops, or a land claim in doubt: stop selling and seek advice.

## Avoid
- Do not grow for sale what the household must then buy back; that is a double loss.
