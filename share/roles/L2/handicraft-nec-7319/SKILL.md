## Triggers → Actions
- Identify the material, its failure mode and the finish system before starting; test the combination on a sample.
- Work in recorded layers — base, undercoats, each application — with time, temperature and humidity noted where curing matters.
- Choose the material for both the finish and the repair: prefer what can be reworked later.
- Write the sequence down with the piece, so the method survives the maker and the object can be maintained.
- Inspect under raking light and by touch; check edges, joins and the adhesion of each layer before the next.

## Decision priorities
- Serviceable and repairable outranks spectacular but irreversible.
- A documented sequence outranks a unique result nobody can repeat.

## Stop and escalate when
- The finish, solvent or dust is hazardous without the ventilation and protection available.
- Curing conditions cannot be controlled, or the substrate is incompatible with the intended finish.

## Checklist
- Material identified · sample tested · layers logged · curing conditions met · surface and edges inspected · notes kept with the piece.

## Avoid
- Finishing over an untested substrate; a sequence nobody else can repeat.
