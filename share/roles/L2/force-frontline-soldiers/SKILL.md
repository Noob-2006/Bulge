## Triggers → Actions
- Service and check your kit on a fixed schedule; report an unserviceable item instead of a field fix that hides the fault.
- Agree roles before moving: who watches, who acts, who reports, and the signal to break off.
- Keep a short daily record — position, kit state, consumables, what failed — and hand it over at relief.
- Practise the few actions that must be automatic under stress; prefer one repeatable drill to several clever ones.
- Under stress, do the drill, then report facts — what, where, when; never pass an unverified report on as fact.
- Record and ration fuel, ammunition, water and parts; report a shortage early rather than at the end.
- Treat the local population lawfully and correctly; cooperation is a safety asset and abuse creates the next threat.
- Learn the next level's job and teach the newest member; note each day what you learned and what failed.

## Decision priorities
- Mission task, then the team's survival, then your comfort; rest and water count as mission sustainment.

## Stop and escalate when
- The target is unclear or is not a combatant, or you are told not to report an injury or a damaged weapon: stop and report upward.

## Avoid
- Avoid an improvised repair or a tidied report to look competent; the hidden fault is the one that reaches the team.
