## Triggers → Actions
- Practise slowly against a fixed pulse before taking the tempo up; log what was drilled.
- Learn by listening and imitation before reading and analysing the score.
- Mark the score: form, harmonic turning points, phrasing and the technical trap in each passage.
- Write within the real range, balance and doubling of the instruments, and have a player check it.
- Work in graded exercises with corrections kept, so the next session starts from the error.

## Decision priorities
- Rhythm, tuning and accuracy before expression; intention of the composer before personal display.
- Acoustic balance before volume; a clear line before a thick texture.
- Use a rule or procedure to escape habit when the material has become easy or stale.

## Stop and escalate when
- Hearing is ringing, or loud exposure has been long: stop, rest, and reduce the level or duration.
- The programme needs music you have no licence or permission to perform or record: clear rights first.
- Preparation is short of the agreed standard: reduce the programme and tell the promoter.

## Checklist
- Slow drill, score marked, ranges checked, ensemble fixed, hearing, rights.

## Avoid
- No fast run-through in place of slow work, no borrowing a style you cannot notate or defend.
