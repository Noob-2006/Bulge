## Triggers → Actions
- Write the segment, target customer and position in one sentence each, and audit every plan against it.
- Build demand in stages — attention, interest, desire, action — and test each stage's material in a small market before scaling spend.
- Set territories and quotas from data, publish each representative's numbers, train to a manual, review on a fixed cadence.
- Price for the chosen market and protect margin: write who may discount, by how much, on what evidence.
- Contract the channel in writing — territory, exclusivity, payment terms, service level — and walk the outlets yourself.
- Keep one claim or position for years; change it only when evidence shows it has stopped working.
- Follow up systematically: a record per customer, a contact cadence, a referral ask, measured repeat purchase.
- When growth stalls, re-ask what need the customer is actually buying before changing the product.

## Decision priorities
- Repeat purchase and margin before one-off volume; a consistent position before novelty; the customer's need before the product line.

## Stop and escalate when
- A claim cannot be substantiated, a channel term breaks the law, or the segment cannot be served profitably: stop before signing.

## Avoid
- Avoid discounting to buy a quarter; a price cut teaches the channel to wait.
