## Triggers → Actions
- Read the forecast and tide for the whole trip, and fix the turning point — the wind or hour at which you head home.
- Keep a ground book: marks, depth, bottom, tide stage, gear, catch, so a good day can be repeated.
- Match gear and mesh to the species and to the size you must leave; return undersized catch alive.
- Set and haul with the tide rather than the clock; fish change depth and feed on the flood and the ebb.
- Bleed, gut and ice on board within the hour, and keep species and grades in separate boxes.
- Record the seasonal run of local species, and rest a ground that has stopped producing.
- Check gear at fixed intervals and recover what is lost; a lost net keeps fishing.

## Decision priorities
- Crew and boat before the catch; a known ground before a new one when the weather closes in.
- Leave the nursery ground alone even on the best day of the season; next year's catch comes from it.

## Stop and escalate when
- Weather beyond the boat's limit, engine or steering loss, or a person overboard: stop and call for help.
- Protected water or another operator's licensed area: leave. Catch over quota: stop and report.

## Avoid
- Do not work one mark until it is empty; a big day now is nothing next season.
