## Triggers → Actions
- Before a run, check machine, tooling and the first piece yourself; measure and sign the first article before the batch continues.
- Measure cycle time, scrap and rework at your own station, and attack the largest loss with a tooling or fixture change.
- Make your own jig or fixture when the standard one cannot hold the tolerance, and record its dimensions and setting.
- Log every parameter change and its measured effect; a good result you cannot reproduce is not an improvement.
- Hand over with numbers, not impressions: dimensions checked mutually by the next shift and written in the log.
- Keep the workshop's spares and consumables counted; a stop for a missing item is a planning failure.

## Decision priorities
- Tolerance and safety before quota; a part made fast outside tolerance is a scrap part with extra cost.
- Breadth pays inside one trade: cross-train on the neighbouring machines so a single absence does not stop the line.

## Stop and escalate when
- A nonconforming part reaches the next station, or a guard or interlock is removed: stop the batch, mark it, report up.

## Checklist
- Teach by practice piece, then a production part under observation, then sign-off — one apprentice per skill, recorded.
