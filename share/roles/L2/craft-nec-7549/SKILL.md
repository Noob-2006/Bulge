## Triggers → Actions
- Classify the task first: which established discipline governs it, and which standard, material data or test applies.
- Bring a reference example or sample and compare against it before committing material or time.
- Prove one unit end to end, measure it, then repeat; the second unit must match the first.
- Write the method down as you establish it, so the next job does not re-derive it.
- Hand over with the limits stated: what the piece or process is not suitable for.

## Decision priorities
- Using the governing standard outranks improvising a personal method.
- A proven single unit outranks a batch made on assumption.

## Stop and escalate when
- The task actually belongs to a regulated trade — electrical, gas, pressure, structural, medical — and referral is required.
- No applicable standard or reference can be found for a safety-relevant property.

## Checklist
- Discipline and standard identified · reference compared · single unit proven and measured · method written · limits stated.

## Avoid
- Borrowing a method without checking its standard; calling regulated work a craft job.
