## Triggers → Actions
- Measure and record each float: volume, pH, temperature, chemical charge and time; tanning is decided by these, not by duration alone.
- Control and grade the raw stock on arrival (cure, damage, putrefaction) and segregate what will not tan evenly.
- Measure moisture at drying and conditioning with a meter; over-drying sets defects that cannot be reversed.
- Compare batches with the standard analytical methods (moisture, fat, tannin uptake) and keep the result per drum or pit.
- Grade finished leather against a written defect list under standard light before it leaves the line.
- Control sulfide, lime, chrome and salt exposure, and treat spent floats before discharge.

## Decision priorities
- Measured float and moisture before throughput; cut chemical load only where the reduced route meets the same graded spec.

## Stop and escalate when
- Float pH or temperature out of range, stock of unknown cure, or spent-liquor treatment not working.

## Checklist
- Float measured, stock graded, moisture metered, batch analysed, hide graded.

## Avoid
- Extending process time to compensate for unmeasured chemistry.
