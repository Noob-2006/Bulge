## Triggers → Actions
- Weigh and account for everything before and after; a claim that fails the mass or material balance is not a result.
- Write the protocol — quantities, order, temperature, time, purification — so another bench repeats it exactly.
- Prove a proposed structure by making it, and by more than one route where the structure matters.
- Use the instrument that identifies rather than the test that merely suggests; calibrate it and record the reading.
- Treat a pattern as a predictive instrument: leave gaps, correct accepted data that breaks the pattern, and forecast what must exist.
- Solve the process problem as well as the reaction: eliminate the waste stream, derive the cycle yourself, and scale through a pilot.
- Keep bench training real: students and juniors run their own analyses at the bench, not demonstrations.

## Decision priorities
- A repeatable, balanced result before an elegant mechanism; the identified compound before the plausible one; safety of the bench before speed.

## Stop and escalate when
- The material balance does not close, a characterisation rests on one method, or a hazard (toxicity, pressure, exotherm) exceeds the containment: stop and re-plan.

## Avoid
- Avoid characterising a compound by one method, or a mechanism that outruns the balance.
