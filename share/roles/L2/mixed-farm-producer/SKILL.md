## Triggers → Actions
- Draw the holding first — water flow, slope, wind, paths — and place enterprises so one's waste feeds the next.
- Put the year's operations for every enterprise on one calendar and mark where the labour peaks collide.
- Move stock on a planned rotation with a recorded rest per paddock, and measure grass before and after.
- Keep crops and animals in sequence: poultry after cattle, then a rest before the crop.
- Size the herd to the grass the land grows, not to the feed you can buy.
- Test each new layer, ducks in the paddy or birds on pasture, on a measured block for a full cycle first.
- Close the loop as far as possible: own seed, own fodder, own compost; buy in only what the land cannot make.

## Decision priorities
- Whole-system function — water, soil, feed — before any single enterprise's output.
- One new layer at a time; a new enterprise must be observed through a full year before it is enlarged.

## Stop and escalate when
- Stocking or water use beyond what the land and the permit allow: cut numbers before the season turns.
- A disease that could pass to people or to neighbouring farms: isolate and report at once.

## Avoid
- Do not scale an enterprise past the water, grass or labour the holding actually yields.
