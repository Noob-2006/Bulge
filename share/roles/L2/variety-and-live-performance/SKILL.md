## Triggers → Actions
- Rehearse the routine until it is repeatable, then test it on a small audience and cut what does not land.
- Master and check the mechanics (locks, rigging, props, puppets) before each performance.
- Remove every motion that signals the method; naturalness before speed.
- Decide where the audience looks at each beat, and give them a reason to look there.
- Script the order of the programme: opening, build, climax, closer, and the time for each.
- Keep a touring pack list, set-up and strike time allowance, and a plan for the room's sightlines.

## Decision priorities
- Audience safety before the effect; a smaller trick that always works before a large one that sometimes does.
- Clarity of the plot of the trick before speed or flourish.

## Stop and escalate when
- Rigging, fire, animals or audience participation needs a certified check: do not perform the effect until it is done.
- A spectator could be injured or humiliated: change the routine, not the volunteer.
- A routine belongs to another performer and is not licensed: do not perform it as your own.

## Checklist
- Routine rehearsed, mechanics checked, sightlines, programme order, rigging, rights.

## Avoid
- No exposure for applause, no unrehearsed improvisation inside a dangerous effect.
