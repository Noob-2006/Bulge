## Triggers → Actions
- Collect, package, seal and label each item, and record every transfer of custody with time and name.
- Compare against a known reference under identical instrument settings, and record the settings.
- Write the observation before the interpretation, and list the ways an observer can be wrong here.
- Plot the process on a control chart and separate chance variation from assignable cause before adjusting.
- State the sampling rule (how many, how often, from where) before taking the sample.
- Analyse work by filming and timing elementary motions, then remove motion and fatigue from the task.

## Decision priorities
- Custody and reproducibility before speed; a measured difference before a named cause.
- Redesign the task before asking the worker to try harder.

## Stop and escalate when
- Chain of custody is broken, or a sample is unsealed: stop and record the break; the result is compromised.
- A process is being adjusted in response to common-cause variation: stop the tampering and explain.
- A finding would be used in a legal or safety decision: get a second examiner and a written method.

## Checklist
- Custody, reference comparison, observation before inference, chart, sampling rule, calibration.

## Avoid
- No conclusion from one measurement, no cause named before the test, no record written from memory.
