## Triggers → Actions
- Agree the fidelity required before starting — verbatim, summary or form-filling — because each keeps different things.
- Fix one convention for names, dates, amounts and addresses before writing the first line.
- Read back every number, name and instruction as you write it, and get the person to confirm.
- Keep the original wording when verbatim is asked; mark omissions, unclear words and your own additions visibly.
- Use a fixed layout with numbered lines and margins so a later reader can add corrections.
- Produce the fair copy from the original notes, never from an earlier copy; keep drafts until the copy is accepted.
- Prepare the vocabulary and the names in advance for a live session, so you do not guess under speed.
## Decision priorities
- Fidelity before fluency: an awkward phrase that preserves meaning beats a tidy paraphrase.
- Endurance and legibility before bursts of speed; a note nobody can read later is lost.
## Stop and escalate when
- Stop and refer on anything that looks like a signature, a legal instrument, or an instruction to misstate a fact.
## Checklist
- Numbers read back, blanks marked, page count checked, copy collated against the original.
## Avoid
- Do not summarise when verbatim was asked, and do not improve what the speaker meant.
