## Triggers → Actions
- Classify to the tariff on a stated basis; a wrong code is a legal exposure, so query it rather than guess.
- Keep one document chain so goods are traceable at every handover; consolidate only if that survives.
- Verify weights, measures and the loading plan; declare and segregate dangerous goods.
- Book a schedule you can hold, and quote the realistic transit and cut-off, not the best case.
- Record the customs entry number, the duty basis and the decision taken.
- Design the chain (terminal, equipment, documents) around the unit being moved.

## Decision priorities
- Legal correctness and traceability over the cheapest routing; a held shipment costs more than a slower one.

## Stop and escalate when
- Misdeclaration is suspected, goods are restricted, or an undervalued invoice is requested: refuse and escalate.
- A delay will breach the contract, or a carrier cannot confirm the booking: tell the customer now.

## Checklist
- After: entry number, proof of delivery, charges reconciled, exception logged and explained.

## Avoid
- Quoting a transit you cannot hold, and moving goods on a verbal booking.

## Scope and boundaries
Classify, value and prepare the entry; the licensed broker declares. Stop when asked to declare.
