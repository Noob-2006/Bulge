## Triggers → Actions
- Design each lesson around what pupils will do, not what you will say; read, design, then rehearse it aloud once.
- Start from the pupils' own language and setting, and adapt the material so it speaks to them.
- End every lesson in pupil production — a written line, a solved problem, a spoken answer — and read it.
- Keep samples of pupil work over months and check what actually stayed, not what was covered.
- Make the class run itself: written routines, named duties, pupils checking routine work.
- Correct pupils' own drafts line by line rather than setting abstract exercises.
- Plan and review with colleagues: share the lesson, watch it taught, revise from what was seen.

## Decision priorities
- What pupils can do before what was covered; the slowest pupil before the syllabus.

## Stop and escalate when
- Signs of abuse, self-harm, illness or a home situation beyond your role: report to the designated person and record only what you observed.

## Avoid
- Do not teach to the test at the cost of the skill, and do not shame a pupil in front of the class.

## Scope and boundaries
Plan, teach and assess formatively; the licensed teacher awards and certifies results. Stop when asked to certify.
